Gospel index / Luke

The words of Jesus in Luke

Read a scene from every chapter of Luke. The opening chapters set the scene for Jesus’ ministry, and the rest follows his words through Galilee, the journey to Jerusalem, the cross and the resurrection.

Start with Nazareth, then read the Good Samaritan and the road to Emmaus. Notice what prompts Jesus to speak at each stop.

The beginnings

Luke 1 · Gabriel and Mary

Luke starts before Jesus begins speaking. Gabriel tells Mary what will happen and names the child. Notice how her question and her final reply frame the announcement.

Read Luke 1:26–38

Luke 2 · Jesus in the temple

Read Mary’s question when she and Joseph find Jesus, then his reply about his Father. The narrator describes what the parents understand and what Mary keeps in mind.

Read Luke 2:41–52

Luke 3 · The baptism and the voice from heaven

Jesus is praying when heaven opens. Notice whose words Luke records here and how the passage leads into the genealogy.

Read Luke 3:21–23

Luke 4 · The reading at Nazareth

Follow Jesus from the public reading in Isaiah to the claim that the words are fulfilled. His examples of Elijah and Elisha come before the crowd’s reaction.

Read Luke 4:16–30

Teaching and healing in Galilee

Luke 5 · The catch of fish

The scene begins with Jesus teaching from Simon’s boat. Read his two instructions about the nets and then his reply to Simon’s reaction.

Read Luke 5:1–11

Luke 6 · Blessings, woes and love of enemies

Read the blessings and woes together before the commands about enemies. Notice whom Jesus addresses as the teaching begins.

Read Luke 6:20–36

Luke 7 · Two debtors at Simon’s table

Simon’s silent judgment comes before Jesus’ story about canceled debts. Listen for how Jesus connects the story to the woman’s actions when he turns toward her.

Read Luke 7:36–50

Luke 8 · The Sower

Read the story and its explanation together. Luke names the seed and closes with a description of people who hear, hold fast and bear fruit with patience.

Read Luke 8:4–15Read the Sower in three Gospels

Luke 9 · Peter’s confession and the call to follow

Jesus asks first what the crowds say and then what the disciples say. Read the prediction of suffering before the command to take up the cross daily.

Read Luke 9:18–27

The journey to Jerusalem

Luke 10 · The Good Samaritan

The lawyer asks who his neighbor is. Jesus ends by asking which person acted as a neighbor. Keep that change in question in view when reading the story.

Read Luke 10:25–37See the Good Samaritan in the parable guide

Luke 11 · The Lord’s Prayer and asking the Father

The disciples’ request comes after they see Jesus pray. Read the prayer, the midnight visitor and Jesus’ closing words about what the Father gives.

Read Luke 11:1–13Compare translations of the prayer

Luke 12 · The rich fool

A dispute over inheritance prompts the warning about possessions. Read the repeated plans of the rich man before the interruption in the story.

Read Luke 12:13–21

Luke 13 · Healing on the Sabbath

Jesus addresses the woman before the synagogue leader objects. Notice his reply about untying an animal and the words he uses to describe the woman.

Read Luke 13:10–17

Luke 14 · Whom to invite and the great banquet

Start with Jesus’ advice to his host. The next remark at the meal prompts the story, so read both before following the change to the guest list.

Read Luke 14:12–24

Luke 15 · The lost sheep, coin and son

All three stories answer a complaint about whom Jesus welcomes. Watch how each story uses rejoicing, especially in the final conversation with the older brother.

Read Luke 15:1–32Jump to the two sons in Luke 15:11–32

Luke 16 · The rich man and Lazarus

Read what the rich man asks for first, then his request about his brothers. Abraham’s final answer brings Moses and the prophets into the discussion.

Read Luke 16:19–31

Luke 17 · Ten healed and one returns

Jesus tells all ten men to go to the priests. Read the questions he asks when only one comes back and notice whom Luke identifies.

Read Luke 17:11–19

Luke 18 · The Pharisee and the tax collector

Verse 9 identifies the people Jesus is addressing. Compare what each man prays and what Jesus says about who goes home justified.

Read Luke 18:9–14

Jerusalem, the cross and resurrection

Luke 19 · Jesus and Zacchaeus

Read Jesus’ invitation to stay and the onlookers’ complaint. The final saying about seeking and saving the lost follows Zacchaeus’s response.

Read Luke 19:1–10

Luke 20 · The question about tribute

Read why the question is asked before Jesus asks to see a coin. Notice how the image on the coin leads to his reply.

Read Luke 20:19–26

Luke 21 · The widow’s offering

Jesus describes the gift in relation to what the widow had. Read the preceding verses about the scribes and widows before comparing the gifts.

Read Luke 21:1–4

Luke 22 · Greatness, service and Peter

At the meal, a dispute about greatness leads Jesus to describe leadership as service. Read on to see what he says to Peter about his failure and return.

Read Luke 22:24–34

Luke 23 · The words from the cross

Luke records a promise to the criminal and a prayer to the Father. Compare how editions present the prayer in verse 34, whose wording varies across manuscripts.

Read Luke 23:32–46Compare the wording at the crucifixion

Luke 24 · On the road to Emmaus

Listen to the travelers explain what they had expected before Jesus answers them. Read how they recognize him and what they remember about the conversation.

Read Luke 24:13–35Compare the greeting of peace in Luke and John
