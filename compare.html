What to look for

The Lord’s Prayer is a good starting point. Compare the wording about debts and forgiveness, then look at the ending in verse 13. The longer ending printed in the KJV reflects a difference in the underlying text. Consult a study edition’s notes when a difference affects how you understand the passage.

Read before and after the selection in the full chapter. Then compare how Luke introduces the prayer in Luke 11:2–4 or try the verse numbering example at John 5:3–5. See translation and source notes. For a parallel Gospel example, compare Mark’s Sower in two editions, then read the Sower’s accounts in three Gospels to see how comparing translations differs from comparing accounts.

If you want translators’ notes or historical background beside the text, compare two print study Bibles.
