About this project

A chronological reading aid for the words of Jesus.

This site gathers statements directly attributed to Jesus Christ in the New Testament and presents them in chronological reading order. It is intended as a study and reference tool, not as a replacement for reading the passages in their full biblical context.

Text and chronology

The wording follows the public-domain King James Version tradition. The Gospel chronology is based on Arthur Hinds’ The Complete Sayings of Jesus (1927), a chronological Gospel harmony, with wording and red-letter boundaries cross-checked against a complete KJV text and structured red-letter data.

Editorial method

The chronological reader is the source collection for the site’s topic guides. A guide begins by locating sayings relevant to a narrowly defined question, then groups representative passages into themes and links each quotation back to the chronological reader. The explanatory paragraphs are editorial summaries; the KJV quotations and Scripture references remain visually distinct so readers can evaluate the summary against the text.

Drafting and site production use software-assisted tools. Before publication, quoted wording, references and reader links are cross-checked against the project’s KJV sayings collection. The goal of the topic pages is to make the source material easier to explore, not to manufacture a large number of pages from loosely related keywords.

When a passage has a disputed speaker boundary, uncertain chronology, parallel Gospel wording, or an interpretive issue that materially affects the guide, the project aims to say so rather than present editorial decisions as manuscript facts.

Editorial limits

Red-letter boundaries are editorial and do not exist in the ancient manuscripts. Some passages—especially John 3:16–21 and Revelation 1:8—have disputed speaker boundaries. This project follows traditional attribution while clearly acknowledging those limits.

Old Testament speeches of “the LORD” are not automatically labeled as sayings of Jesus, because doing so requires a theological inference beyond explicit narrative attribution.

Source files

Primary chronological source: Arthur Hinds, The Complete Sayings of Jesus (1927). Bible wording: public-domain KJV/1769 tradition.
