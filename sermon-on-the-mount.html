Gospels / Matthew / Sermon on the Mount

Sermon on the Mount Bible study

Read all of Matthew 5–7 in six sessions. Each session gives you a passage, something to watch for, and questions you can use on your own or with a group.

Blessings, salt and lightRighteousness in relationshipsGiving, prayer and fastingTreasure, allegiance and daily needsJudging, asking and doing goodTwo ways and two builders

How to use the study

Read the whole selection before answering the questions. For a group, read aloud and give each person a moment to find a verse that supports an observation. End by naming one question to carry into the next session.

Want a paper copy? Use your browser’s Print command. The print layout includes space for notes.

  1. Blessings, salt and light

    Read Matthew 5:1–16

    Watch who gathers and who is described as blessed. Then notice how Jesus moves from the blessings to directly addressing the listeners as salt and light.

    Discuss or write What do the images of salt, a city and a lamp ask of the listeners? What reason does verse 16 give for letting others see their good works?

  2. Righteousness in relationships

    Read Matthew 5:17–48

    Read verses 17–20 before the examples about anger, adultery, divorce, oaths, retaliation and enemies. Notice which situations Jesus uses to describe conduct as well as motives.

    Discuss or write In verses 21–26, what actions does Jesus describe before worship and on the way to court? How does the call to love enemies develop the section’s concern with our treatment of others?

  3. Giving, prayer and fasting

    Read Matthew 6:1–18

    Read the opening warning about being seen, then mark the repeated phrases as Jesus turns to giving, praying and fasting. Read the lines on forgiveness immediately after the prayer.

    Discuss or write How does the audience for each practice change? Which request in the prayer receives further attention in verses 14–15?

  4. Treasure, allegiance and daily needs

    Read Matthew 6:19–34

    Follow the sequence from treasure to the eye, two masters and daily needs. Read verse 25 with verse 24 so you can see what the transition is doing.

    Discuss or write How do the birds and lilies support the teaching about daily needs? What does Jesus tell the listeners to seek in verse 33?

  5. Judging, asking and doing good

    Read Matthew 7:1–12

    Read the image of the beam through the instruction in verse 5. Then follow the movement from asking the Father for good things to treating other people as you would want them to treat you.

    Discuss or write What must the person with the beam do first? Read verse 12 alongside Matthew 5:17 and compare what Jesus says about the Law and the Prophets.

  6. Two ways and two builders

    Read Matthew 7:13–29

    List the paired images and contrasts in the closing section: gates and roads, trees and fruit, and houses on different foundations. Keep reading through the crowd’s response.

    Discuss or write What experience do the two builders share? What difference does Jesus name, and what does the crowd notice when he finishes?

Read the sermon as a whole

Return to the opening in Matthew 5:1–2, then read the crowd’s response in Matthew 7:28–29. Which images or instructions connect two of your sessions? Continue with Matthew 8 or use the passage index to revisit one teaching.

For a shorter companion reading, compare the blessings, instructions and closing images in Luke 6:17–49. Make a note of where Luke’s setting or wording differs.
