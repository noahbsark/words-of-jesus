Who is speaking in these Bible passages?

One verse can include a question, a reply and a narrator’s description. Follow these examples to see where one voice ends and the next begins.

Start with the lawyer’s question in Luke 10. Find the named speaker and the reply, then notice where a character speaks inside Jesus’ story.

Speaker labels are available in selected passages in the Bible reader and in View Bible text on a saying card. If a label names more than one voice, read the whole verse to see where it changes.

When a verse contains more than one voice

The lawyer’s question and Jesus’ ending

Start with the lawyer’s question and note who gives the summary of the law in verse 27. In verse 37, the lawyer answers first and Jesus replies. The Samaritan also speaks inside the story in verse 35.

Read Luke 10:25–37

Two similar questions to Mary

The angels ask Mary why she is weeping in verse 13. Jesus asks a similar question in verse 15. Mary answers both, and what she knows changes when Jesus calls her by name.

Read John 20:11–18

Peter’s objection and Jesus’ reply

Verse 8 contains both Peter’s refusal and Jesus’ answer. In verse 11 the narrator explains an earlier remark and repeats part of it. Compare that repetition with the original exchange in verse 10.

Read John 13:6–17

When another speaker quotes Jesus

Paul recalls a saying

In verse 35 Paul tells the elders to remember Jesus’ words about giving and receiving. Cite Acts if you want someone to find the line. Acts leaves the original occasion unspecified.

Read Acts 20:32–38

The leaders report a prediction

Verse 63 is addressed to Pilate by the chief priests and Pharisees. They recall a prediction Jesus made. Read Mark 8:31 for a scene where Jesus himself teaches that he would rise.

Read Matthew 27:62–66Read Mark 8:31

When editions differ

Where does the reply to Nicodemus end?

John introduces Jesus’ reply in verse 10. Some editions continue the speech through verse 21. Others treat verses 16–21 as the narrator’s explanation. Cite John 3:16 if you are quoting the verse and identify the edition if its quotation marks matter to your point.

Read John 3:9–21Read Darrell Bock’s discussion of John 3

The prayer from the cross

Where an edition includes the prayer in verse 34, Jesus speaks and the narrator then describes the casting of lots. Manuscript notes are important here because editions can include, bracket or footnote the prayer.

Read Luke 23:32–43Read the NET textual note

The commission in Mark’s longer ending

Verse 15 introduces instructions attributed to Jesus in the longer ending. Verse 19 returns to narration. Consult the notes in the edition you are using if you are quoting this passage alongside Matthew’s commission.

Read Mark 16:8–20Compare how the editions print the ending

For another speaker change in John, read Thomas’s question before Jesus’ reply about the way. The full chapter labels the exchange.

Check another passage

Read from the last named speaker through the reply. If an edition puts quotation marks or a footnote in a different place, keep its wording and reference with your quotation. Use the passage finder or the guide to familiar sayings to find the source.
