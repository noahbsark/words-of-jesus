The seven “I am” sayings of Jesus in John

Read the seven familiar images in their own settings, from the bread of life and the light of the world to the shepherd, the way and the vine.

The seven images and their settings

The bread of life

The crowd has followed Jesus after the feeding and asks for a sign. Read its reference to manna and its request for bread before Jesus explains what it means to come to him.

Read John 6:35Read the setting in John 6:25–40

The light of the world

The image of light leads to an objection about Jesus’ testimony. Read the objection and his reply. In John 9, Jesus uses the image again before healing the man born blind.

Read John 8:12Read the setting in John 8:12–20Read the second use of the image in John 9:1–7

The door or gate

The narrator says the listeners did not understand the first illustration. Jesus then identifies himself with the entrance to the sheepfold. Compare the wording at verse 9, which repeats the image.

Read John 10:7Read the setting in John 10:1–10Compare “door” and “gate”

The good shepherd

The shepherd keeps caring for the sheep when the hired hand leaves. Notice how the image moves from knowing the sheep to willingly laying down his life.

Read John 10:11Read the setting in John 10:11–18

The resurrection and the life

Martha already expects a resurrection on the last day. After Jesus answers, he asks whether she believes. Read Martha’s reply before continuing to the tomb scene.

Read John 11:25Read the setting in John 11:17–27

The way, the truth and the life

Thomas asks how the disciples can know the way if they do not know where Jesus is going. Philip’s next question is about seeing the Father. Both questions help locate the saying within the farewell conversation.

Read John 14:6Read the setting in John 14:1–11Compare the question and reply

The true vine

The image names the vine, the branches and the one who tends them. Read how bearing fruit and remaining in Jesus lead into the instruction to love one another.

Read John 15:1Read the setting in John 15:1–17

Other uses of “I am” in John

The seven entries use images or descriptions. In John 4:26, Jesus identifies himself in reply to the woman’s statement about the Messiah. John 8:58 comes during a dispute about Abraham. Read each conversation before comparing how editions print the identification.

Read John 4:19–26Read John 8:48–59
