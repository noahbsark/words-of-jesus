Passion week guide

The seven sayings of Jesus from the cross.

The “Seven Sayings” or “Seven Last Words” are a traditional way of gathering Jesus’ spoken words during the crucifixion from all four Gospels. No single Gospel records all seven, so the familiar sequence is a harmony of Matthew, Mark, Luke and John.

Why are there seven if the reader has more entries?

The chronological reader treats distinct lines as separate speaking entries. The traditional Seven Sayings groups “Woman, behold thy son” and “Behold thy mother” together as one saying, producing the familiar count of seven.

1. “Father, forgive them”

Luke places this prayer at the crucifixion as Jesus asks forgiveness for those acting against him.

“Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do.”
Luke 23:34

2. “To day shalt thou be with me in paradise”

Jesus answers the repentant criminal who asks to be remembered when Jesus comes into his kingdom.

“Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.”
Luke 23:43

3. “Woman, behold thy son ... Behold thy mother”

John records Jesus addressing his mother and the disciple whom he loved, entrusting them to one another. The reader stores the two direct addresses as adjacent entries.

“Woman, behold thy son!”

4. “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?”

Matthew and Mark record this cry using words from Psalm 22, pointing readers toward the psalm’s language of suffering.

“My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?”
Matthew 27:46; Mark 15:34

5. “I thirst”

John explicitly connects this saying with Scripture being fulfilled immediately before Jesus receives vinegar.
