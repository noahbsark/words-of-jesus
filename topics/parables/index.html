The ranges sometimes include the question that prompted the story or the response that follows. When two Gospels tell a similar story, read each setting on its own before comparing details.

The kingdom and its growth

The Sower

Read the story and its explanation in the same account. Luke explicitly identifies the seed as the word of God. Matthew emphasizes hearing and understanding, while Luke closes with bearing fruit patiently.

Matthew 13:1–23Mark 4:1–20Luke 8:4–15

The Wheat and the Weeds

The servants want to pull up the weeds immediately. Read the owner’s reason for waiting, then see how Jesus answers the disciples’ request for an explanation.

Matthew 13:24–30Matthew 13:36–43

The Growing Seed

Mark distinguishes the farmer’s sowing and harvesting from the growth he cannot explain. Read it alongside the mustard seed that follows.

Mark 4:26–29

The Mustard Seed

Compare where the seed is planted and what shelters the birds. Luke places this comparison after a Sabbath healing. Matthew and Mark put it near other seed stories.

Matthew 13:31–32Mark 4:30–32Luke 13:10–19

The Leaven

Watch how the comparison moves from leaven hidden in the flour to its effect on the entire batch. Matthew and Luke both place it after the mustard seed.

Matthew 13:31–33Luke 13:18–21

The Hidden Treasure and the Pearl

Both people sell what they have. One comes across a treasure, while the other is already looking for fine pearls.

Matthew 13:44–46

Mercy, forgiveness and prayer

The Good Samaritan

Start with the lawyer’s question about who counts as a neighbor. At the end Jesus asks which traveler acted as a neighbor.

Luke 10:25–37

The Lost Sheep

Luke opens with a complaint that Jesus welcomes sinners and closes the story with joy over repentance. Matthew places the similar image in a warning about overlooking the little ones.

Luke 15:1–7Matthew 18:10–14

The Lost Coin

Listen for the call to rejoice after the search. It repeats a pattern from the sheep story and prepares the reader for the last story in Luke 15.

Luke 15:8–10

The Prodigal Son

Read past the reunion to the older brother’s refusal to join the celebration. The father speaks to both sons, and the older son’s reply to his invitation is left untold.

Luke 15:11–32

The Unforgiving Servant

Peter’s question sets up the story. Compare the first debt, the much smaller second debt, and the way each creditor responds to a plea for time.

Matthew 18:21–35

The Pharisee and the Tax Collector

Verse 9 identifies the audience. Compare what each man says to God and notice Jesus’ explanation of who goes home justified.

Luke 18:9–14

Hearing and responding

The Two Builders

Both builders hear Jesus’ words. Compare how Matthew describes the two foundations and how Luke describes digging before the flood comes.

Matthew 7:24–29Luke 6:46–49

The Great Banquet

Read the meal conversation first. Notice whose invitations Jesus has just discussed and how the guest list changes after the excuses.

Luke 14:12–24

The Workers in the Vineyard

The workers agree on a wage, but the payment order exposes different expectations. The story follows Jesus’ answer to Peter about what the disciples will receive.

Matthew 19:27–30Matthew 20:1–16

The Two Sons

The story follows an exchange about Jesus’ authority. Read his application to the religious leaders after they answer his question.

Matthew 21:23–32

The Ten Virgins

Follow the sequence of delay, sudden arrival and a closed door. The closing instruction ties the story to the preceding discussion about readiness.

Matthew 25:1–13

The Talents

The word talent refers to a sum of money in the story. Compare what the three servants do during the master’s absence and what each says when he returns.

Matthew 25:14–30
