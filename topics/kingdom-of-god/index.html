What did Jesus say about the kingdom of God?

The kingdom is one of the most persistent themes in Jesus’ recorded teaching. He announces its nearness, explains it through parables, describes who receives it, calls people to seek it first, and speaks of its present and future dimensions.

At a glance

  • Jesus begins public preaching with repentance and the nearness of the kingdom.
  • New birth is connected with seeing and entering the kingdom.
  • Parables picture the kingdom as seed, leaven, treasure, a pearl and a net.
  • The kingdom reorders priorities: seek it before anxious accumulation.
  • Jesus also speaks about judgment, readiness and the future coming of the Son of man.

The kingdom is announced as near

In the early Galilean ministry, Jesus’ proclamation is a call to response rather than mere information. Repentance and belief stand beside the announcement that God’s kingdom has drawn near.

“The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of heaven is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel.”
Mark 1:15; Matthew 4:17

New birth and the kingdom

In the conversation with Nicodemus, Jesus says that seeing and entering the kingdom requires being born again—or born from above—and being born of water and the Spirit.

“Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.”
John 3:3

The kingdom can begin small

Mustard seed and leaven are compact images of growth. Jesus uses ordinary processes that begin modestly and reach beyond their starting point.

“The kingdom of heaven is like unto a grain of mustard seed ...”
Matthew 13:31–32

The kingdom is worth reordering everything for

The hidden treasure and pearl of great price emphasize value and joyful priority. Recognizing what has been found changes what the person is willing to relinquish.

“The kingdom of heaven is like unto a treasure hid in a field ... for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field.”
Matthew 13:44

Seek the kingdom before anxious accumulation

In teaching about food, clothing and worry, Jesus redirects attention from anxious pursuit toward God’s kingdom while explicitly saying the Father knows material needs.

“But rather seek ye the kingdom of God; and all these things shall be added unto you.”
Luke 12:31

Not simply “here” or “there”

When questioned about when the kingdom would come, Jesus says it does not arrive in a way that permits people simply to point “here” or “there.” The KJV reads “the kingdom of God is within you”; interpreters differ over whether the phrase is better understood as “within” or “in your midst.”

“The kingdom of God cometh not with observation ... for, behold, the kingdom of God is within you.”
Luke 17:20–21
