Gospel index / Matthew

The words of Jesus in Matthew

Read a scene from each of Matthew’s 28 chapters. The first chapters set the scene for Jesus’ ministry. The rest follows his teaching from Galilee to Jerusalem and the commission after the resurrection.

Beginnings and the call to follow

Matthew 1 · The announcement to Joseph

Matthew gives the words of the angel to Joseph before the birth. Compare the name the angel gives with the meaning of Emmanuel in Matthew’s quotation.

Read Matthew 1:18–25

Matthew 2 · The visitors from the east

Track who asks about the child and what each person intends to do when he is found. The travelers leave by another route after the warning at the end.

Read Matthew 2:1–12

Matthew 3 · Jesus and John at the baptism

John objects before Jesus answers. Pay attention to which words are spoken by Jesus and which come from the voice after the baptism.

Read Matthew 3:13–17

Matthew 4 · The temptations in the wilderness

For each reply, listen for the passage Jesus quotes and the question or demand it answers. Compare the order of the last two scenes with Luke.

Read Matthew 4:1–11Compare Matthew and Luke’s order

The Sermon on the Mount

Matthew 5 · The Beatitudes, salt and light

Read the blessings as a sequence. Notice when Jesus changes to addressing his listeners directly and how the images of salt and light follow.

Read Matthew 5:3–16Browse the Sermon on the Mount passage by passage

Matthew 6 · Prayer and the Father

Read the instructions about where and how to pray, then the prayer itself. Retain the two verses on forgiveness after it.

Read Matthew 6:5–15Compare translations of the prayerRead the teaching about daily needs in Matthew 6:25–34

Matthew 7 · The two builders

Both builders hear the words. Compare what they do afterwards and read the crowd’s response to the whole sermon.

Read Matthew 7:24–29

Healing, sending and teaching in Galilee

Matthew 8 · The centurion’s request

The officer describes his own authority to explain why he trusts Jesus to speak a word. Read Jesus’ reply to the people following and then his final words to the centurion.

Read Matthew 8:5–13

Matthew 9 · Matthew’s call and the meal

Notice who questions Jesus’ disciples about the meal. Jesus answers with the image of a physician and then tells his questioners what to go and learn.

Read Matthew 9:9–13

Matthew 10 · Sending the twelve

Read whom Jesus sends the twelve to and what he tells them to say. Notice how the instructions change depending on whether a household welcomes them.

Read Matthew 10:5–16

Matthew 11 · An invitation to the burdened

Read the thanksgiving and the words about the Father and Son before the invitation to come. Keep the yoke and rest together in Jesus’ closing image.

Read Matthew 11:25–30

Matthew 12 · Grain on the Sabbath

Begin with the objection about the disciples’ conduct. Follow how Jesus uses David, the priests and mercy in his reply before the statement about the Sabbath.

Read Matthew 12:1–8

Matthew 13 · The wheat and the weeds

Read what the servants want to do and why the householder tells them to wait. Later in the chapter the disciples ask Jesus to explain the story.

Read Matthew 13:24–30Read Jesus’ explanation in Matthew 13:36–43

Matthew 14 · Walking on the water

Read what Jesus says to the group first, then what he says to Peter. The scene closes with the wind ceasing and a response from those in the boat.

Read Matthew 14:22–33

Matthew 15 · The Canaanite woman

Follow the woman’s repeated appeals, including the reply about crumbs. Compare Matthew’s closing words about faith with Jesus’ response in Mark.

Read Matthew 15:21–28Read Mark’s account of the woman’s request

The way to Jerusalem and life together

Matthew 16 · Peter’s confession and the cross

Notice the shift from Peter’s answer about Jesus’ identity to his objection when Jesus speaks of suffering. Then read the instruction Jesus gives to his disciples.

Read Matthew 16:13–28

Matthew 17 · The transfiguration

Read who speaks from the cloud before Jesus tells the disciples to rise. The instruction on the way down includes a time limit.

Read Matthew 17:1–9

Matthew 18 · The unforgiving servant

Peter’s question sets the scene. Read the requests for patience in both encounters and notice how they are answered.

Read Matthew 18:21–35

Matthew 19 · The man with many possessions

Follow the man’s questions before his departure. Jesus’ next words are to the disciples, who ask who can be saved.

Read Matthew 19:16–26

Matthew 20 · The workers in the vineyard

Track the wage agreement and the order in which the workers are paid. Notice which workers bring a complaint and how the landowner answers them.

Read Matthew 20:1–16

Jerusalem, the cross and resurrection

Matthew 21 · Authority and the two sons

The leaders ask Jesus about his authority. Read their reasoning about John and compare it with the answer they give after the story of the two sons.

Read Matthew 21:23–32

Matthew 22 · The greatest commandment

A lawyer’s question introduces Jesus’ two replies about love. Read the closing sentence about the law and the prophets before comparing Mark.

Read Matthew 22:34–40Read Matthew and Mark side by side

Matthew 23 · Teaching and practice

Notice that Jesus speaks to the crowds and the disciples. Compare what he says about burdens, public honor and service before the chapter turns to the woes.

Read Matthew 23:1–12

Matthew 24 · The unknown hour and watchfulness

Read the statement about who knows the hour before the images of Noah, a householder and a servant. The examples describe how people act while waiting.

Read Matthew 24:36–51

Matthew 25 · The sheep and the goats

Listen for the questions both groups ask the king and the repeated answer about what they did to others. The passage follows two teachings about readiness.

Read Matthew 25:31–46

Matthew 26 · Prayer in Gethsemane

Compare what Jesus asks of the disciples with what he asks of the Father. Follow how the prayer and the disciples’ sleep recur before the arrest.

Read Matthew 26:36–46Compare the prayer in Matthew and Mark

Matthew 27 · The cry from the cross

Read Matthew’s translation of the cry before the bystanders’ response. The wording echoes Psalm 22, and the scene continues through the centurion’s response.

Read Matthew 27:45–54Read the sayings from the cross across four Gospels

Matthew 28 · The commission in Galilee

Read what the disciples do at the meeting before Jesus speaks. Follow the movement from his authority to the instructions and then the closing promise.

Read Matthew 28:16–20
