Gospel index / Mark

The words of Jesus in Mark

Follow Mark from the opening proclamation in Galilee to the message at the tomb. Each of the 16 chapters has a reading prompt and a link into the full chapter.

For a short first pass, read the opening proclamation, Peter’s confession and the crucifixion. At each stop, notice what someone says about Jesus.

Jump to the storm, greatness and service, Gethsemane or the tomb.

Teaching in Galilee

Mark 1 · The kingdom is near

Read the announcement about the kingdom and the call to repent and believe. Notice what the fishermen leave behind in the scene that follows.

Read Mark 1:14–20

Mark 2 · The man lowered through the roof

Jesus speaks about forgiveness before he tells the man to rise. Read the scribes’ objection to see why the order matters.

Read Mark 2:1–12

Mark 3 · Healing on the Sabbath

Jesus’ question asks the onlookers to choose between doing good and doing harm. Watch how their silence and their later response frame the healing.

Read Mark 3:1–6

Mark 4 · Calming the storm

Notice what the disciples ask Jesus during the storm and what they ask each other after it. Read the command to the sea between those two questions.

Read Mark 4:35–41Compare with Luke’s account

Mark 5 · Jairus and the woman who touches Jesus’ garment

Jairus asks Jesus to help his daughter, and the woman’s encounter interrupts the journey. Read Jesus’ words about faith in both parts of the story.

Read Mark 5:21–43Jump to the exchange in Mark 5:30–34

Mark 6 · Feeding the five thousand

Start with Jesus’ invitation for the apostles to rest. Then notice how the disciples respond when he tells them to give the crowd something to eat.

Read Mark 6:30–44

Mark 7 · Jesus and the Syrophoenician woman

Follow the exchange about children’s bread and the woman’s reply about crumbs. Mark records Jesus’ response to what she says before she returns home.

Read Mark 7:24–30

On the way to Jerusalem

Mark 8 · Peter’s confession and the call to follow

Jesus’ question about who he is leads to a prediction of suffering. Read Peter’s response before Jesus turns to the crowd and speaks about taking up the cross.

Read Mark 8:27–38

Mark 9 · Who is the greatest?

The disciples are silent when Jesus asks about their argument. Put his instruction to become last and serve all beside his action of welcoming a child.

Read Mark 9:30–37

Mark 10 · Greatness and service

James and John ask for places of honor. Follow Jesus’ answer about his cup and the way he redirects the argument toward service.

Read Mark 10:35–45Continue with Bartimaeus in Mark 10:46–52

Teaching in Jerusalem

Mark 11 · The fig tree and the temple

Mark places the temple scene between the two encounters with the tree. Read the temple quotation and then listen for Jesus’ instructions about prayer and forgiveness.

Read Mark 11:12–25

Mark 12 · The greatest commandment

Mark records the scribe’s question, Jesus’ answer about love, and the scribe’s reply. Notice what Jesus says after hearing that reply.

Read Mark 12:28–34Compare with Matthew’s account

Mark 13 · The temple and the disciples’ question

Begin with the remark about the temple buildings and the private question it prompts. Read the warnings before continuing to the rest of the chapter.

Read Mark 13:1–13

The arrest, cross and tomb

Mark 14 · Prayer in Gethsemane

Read what Jesus asks of the disciples before his prayer. Then follow the repeated returns to them and the change when he announces that the hour has come.

Read Mark 14:32–42Compare the prayer with Matthew

Mark 15 · The cry from the cross

Mark gives the cry and its translation. Read the bystanders’ reaction and the centurion’s words after Jesus dies.

Read Mark 15:33–39Read the sayings from the cross across four Gospels

Mark 16 · The message at the tomb

The young man at the tomb speaks in this scene and tells the women to tell the disciples and Peter. Compare how editions present the later verses before quoting the commission in Mark 16:15.

Read Mark 16:1–8Compare how editions present Mark 16:8–18
