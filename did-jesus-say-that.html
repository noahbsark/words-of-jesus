Familiar wording and its source

“Do unto others as you would have them do unto you”

Jesus teaches this principle in Matthew and Luke. The familiar English line is a short paraphrase. Matthew connects the instruction with the law and the prophets. Luke places it alongside commands about loving enemies and doing good.

Matthew 7:7–12Luke 6:27–36

For Matthew’s setting, use the Matthew 7 study session and read through the words about the Law and the Prophets.

“The truth will set you free”

The BSB has this wording in John 8:32. The KJV says the truth will “make you free.” Read the preceding verse for whom Jesus addresses and the condition about continuing in his word.

John 8:31–36Compare the wording

“Faith can move mountains”

This summarizes the reply Jesus gives after the disciples ask why they could not cast out a spirit. Matthew uses the image of a mountain in this scene. In Luke’s mustard-seed saying, the image is a tree.

Matthew 17:14–20Luke 17:5–6

Check who is speaking

“Money is the root of all evil”

First Timothy 6:10 contains the familiar line in a fuller form. It concerns the love of money and appears in a letter. To read Jesus’ own teaching on possessions, start with the inheritance question in Luke 12.

1 Timothy 6:6–10Luke 12:13–21

“I can do all things through Christ”

This line comes from Philippians. Paul is describing what he has learned in plenty and in hunger. Read the sentences before the familiar line to see the circumstances he has in mind.

Philippians 4:10–14

“God helps those who help themselves”

This proverb has no corresponding saying of Jesus in the Gospel texts on this site. Two passages worth reading on their own are Jesus’ teaching about asking the Father and his description of the Father’s care for daily needs.

Matthew 7:7–11Matthew 6:25–34

Read beyond the familiar line

“Judge not”

Jesus opens this part of the sermon with a warning about judging. Read through the beam and speck illustration for the role of examining your own conduct. John 7 records a separate exchange about judgment in a Sabbath dispute.

Matthew 7:1–5John 7:19–24

“Love your neighbor as yourself”

Jesus gives this as the second commandment in his reply to the scribe. The wording comes from Leviticus. Read Jesus’ first commandment and the scribe’s response to see where the quotation sits in the exchange.

Mark 12:28–34Leviticus 19:17–18

“Where two or three are gathered”

Jesus says this in a sequence that begins with how to respond when someone sins against you. Read through the references to witnesses, the community and agreement before applying the sentence on its own.

Matthew 18:15–20
