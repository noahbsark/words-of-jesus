The words of Jesus in John

Read a selected scene from each of John’s 21 chapters. Each passage opens where the conversation begins, with the full chapter available around it.

Meeting Jesus

John 1 · The first disciples

The first question is about what the two disciples are seeking. Follow how the invitations to come and see move from Jesus to his new followers.

Read John 1:35–51

John 2 · The wedding at Cana

Start with Mary’s observation that the wine has run out. Notice who hears Jesus’ instructions and who knows where the wine came from.

Read John 2:1–11

John 3 · Nicodemus comes by night

Follow Nicodemus’s questions about birth and Jesus’ answers about the Spirit. Editions may place the end of Jesus’ speech in different places.

Read John 3:1–21Compare the wording in two translations

John 4 · The Samaritan woman

Track the conversation from a request for water to questions about worship and the Messiah. Read the disciples’ return and the town’s response later in the chapter.

Read John 4:4–26

If the reference to “this mountain” raises a question, see a study Bible focused on historical and geographic background.

John 5 · Healing at the pool

Read the question to the man before the command to rise. The healing leads to a dispute about the Sabbath and Jesus’ relationship to the Father.

Read John 5:1–18

Signs and public teaching

John 6 · The bread of life

Notice what the crowd asks for after the feeding. Jesus moves the discussion from the bread they ate to the bread the Father gives.

Read John 6:25–40Compare the bread of life passage

John 7 · Living water at the feast

Jesus’ invitation comes on the last day of the feast. The narrator explains the image in the next verse, then the crowd disagrees about who Jesus is.

Read John 7:37–44

John 8 · The light of the world

The response to the image of light is a challenge about testimony. Keep this exchange distinct from the preceding story of the woman accused of adultery, whose placement editions mark differently.

Read John 8:12–20

John 9 · Sight and blindness

Read this exchange after the man has been questioned and cast out. The chapter moves from physical sight to who claims to see.

Read John 9:35–41

John 10 · The Good Shepherd

Jesus uses both the door and the shepherd as images. Notice how his description of the shepherd moves from knowing the sheep to laying down his life.

Read John 10:7–18

John 11 · Jesus and Martha

Martha speaks about resurrection at the last day. Notice how Jesus’ reply changes the focus of the conversation before the scene moves to the tomb.

Read John 11:17–27Continue at the tomb in John 11:38–44

John 12 · The hour has come

Start with the request from the Greeks who want to see Jesus. Follow how Jesus answers with the image of a seed that must fall into the ground.

Read John 12:20–36

The meal, farewell and prayer

John 13 · Washing the disciples’ feet

Read Peter’s initial refusal and Jesus’ answer before reading the instruction to do as he has done. Later in this chapter, look for the new commandment.

Read John 13:1–17Read the new commandment in John 13:31–35

John 14 · Jesus answers Thomas and Philip

Read each question before the reply. Thomas asks about the way, while Philip asks to see the Father.

Read John 14:1–11

John 15 · The vine and the branches

Follow the repeated language of abiding from the vine image into the instruction to love one another.

Read John 15:1–17

John 16 · Sorrow and joy

Start with the disciples’ confusion about “a little while.” Notice how Jesus uses childbirth to explain a change from sorrow to joy.

Read John 16:16–24Read Jesus’ words about the Spirit in John 16:7–15

John 17 · The prayer for his followers

The prayer moves from the people who are already with Jesus to those who will believe through their words. Watch for what he asks for each group.

Read John 17:6–23

Trial, cross and resurrection

John 18 · Jesus and Pilate

Read Pilate’s question about kingship before Jesus speaks of his kingdom and his purpose in bearing witness to the truth.

Read John 18:33–38

John 19 · Jesus’ words from the cross

Notice who stands nearby before Jesus speaks to his mother and the beloved disciple. The narrator connects the words about thirst with the fulfillment of Scripture.

Read John 19:25–30See the sayings from all four Gospels

John 20 · Jesus and Mary Magdalene

Mary first hears a question and takes Jesus for the gardener. Watch what changes when he calls her by name and what he asks her to do next.

Read John 20:11–18Compare the greeting of peace in John and LukeRead the exchange with Thomas in John 20:24–29

John 21 · Jesus and Peter by the sea

Compare the three questions about love and the instructions that follow. The narrator then corrects a rumor about what Jesus said concerning the beloved disciple.

Read John 21:15–23
